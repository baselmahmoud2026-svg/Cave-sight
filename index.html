CaveSight looks for places on Earth that match a real lava tube on the Moon. It scores Earth sites against three real targets (a lunar lava-tube pit, the Martian skylights of Arsia Mons and the lunar south pole) using open NASA and ESA data, and shows the results on maps of Earth, the Moon and Mars.

01 / The match

Two worlds.
One 45-metre tube.

A 2024 radar study found a conduit about 45 m wide under a pit on the Moon. A 2024 field survey found passages up to 45 m wide inside Umm Jirsan, a lava tube in Saudi Arabia. The width match comes from the literature; a surface elevation model cannot measure it. CaveSight asks a different question: given only coordinates and open data, with no labels, does the pipeline rank Umm Jirsan first?

    06 / Method

    Open box.
    Audited after.

    No neural networks and no tuned weights. Standardise the readings, measure the distance to the Moon in three separate pillars, combine them, and only then reveal the labels to test the hypotheses.

    Coordinates→Snow filter→Terrain window→Three pillars→Audit
    01

    Blind input

    The method is frozen and SHA-256 hashed before any download. Coordinates go in as (id, lat, lon) only; labels stay sealed until the ranking is locked.

    02

    Snow filter

    Bare-earth albedo is the darkest of 12 monthly NASA POWER values. A high monthly SD flags winter snow (0.108 at Craters of the Moon).

    03

    Terrain window

    A 3×3 Copernicus GLO-90 grid at about 150 m spacing. Relief = highest − lowest node. Target: 45 m relief, 15° slope.

    04

    Three pillars

    Regolith, topography and environment are scored separately, then combined with a geometric mean, so climate cannot outvote geology.

    05

    Audit

    Labels revealed. Mann-Whitney U test, Monte Carlo noise test, 232 alternative setups. Results reported exactly as printed.

    H0 · ANCHOR FOUND

    Does the pipeline find Umm Jirsan?

    Yes, at rank #1. Treated as a sanity check, not proof, because the anchor defines the target.

    H1 · VOLCANIC > SAND

    Do all volcanic sites beat the sand?

    No. Algodones Dunes (#5) outranks Craters of the Moon (#6) and El Pinacate (#8).

    p = 0.2857Fail
    H2 · GEOLOGY ONLY

    Does geology alone separate them?

    Closer: only El Pinacate falls below the best control. Its large climate pixel mixes in the surrounding dunes.

    p = 0.1905Fail
    H3 · DARKER BASALT

    Are volcanic sites darker?

    Again only El Pinacate falls below. With 5 vs 2 sites the smallest possible p is 0.048, so near-misses cannot pass.

    p = 0.0952Fail
    SPECIFICATION CURVE · 232 SETUPS82.8%Umm Jirsan still #1

    Every analysis choice from the notebook's history, re-run. Perfect volcanic-vs-sand separation in only 6.0% (median p = 0.388); the geology-only model separates best (16.7%).

    MONTE CARLO · 1,000 DRAWS100%Umm Jirsan P(rank 1)

    Inputs jittered by stated measurement errors. Its score stays within 66.7–73.7%. Perfect separation of volcanic and sand: 0.0% of draws.

    SITE-SELECTION SENSITIVITYp = 0.61With all 12 sites

    Adding back the 4 dropped sites keeps Umm Jirsan #1 on the composite, but the geology index then ranks the Amazon rainforest control first. Geology proxies alone are not enough.

    In plain words: the tool reliably recognises a known lunar-scale lava tube from open data, and it cannot yet prove that volcanic terrain and desert sand are always separable, with only eight sites and 55–110 km climate pixels. It also does not show that any site contains a tube; a surface DEM cannot see one. The fix is more sites and finer data (30 m HLS, 60 m EMIT, thermal buffering), not tuning the weights.

    04 · SHELTER PHYSICS

    Roofs and thermal buffering

    A tube is a shelter only if its roof holds and its inside stays steady. In a simple beam model a 45 m lunar span equals a 7.4 m span on Earth. Thermal buffering B compares the swing at the entrance with the swing of the surface around it.
